Just‑In‑Time
Scene Description

A resolution layer beneath USD composition that turns a stage from a file you open into a query you evaluate — and gives the scene graph a version identity it has never had.

01 — Thesis

USD is already the evaluator. Only its inputs are rigid.

USD's composition engine is a pure functional evaluator. LIVRPS — Local, Inherits, VariantSets, References, Payloads, Specializes — is a deterministic function from a layer stack to a composed stage. Open the same layers, get the same stage. Pixar already built the hard part.

What is rigid is not the evaluator but that its inputs are static files on disk. Someone must publish hero_car_v037.usd before anyone can reference it, and the reference is a path — so it points at a thing that already exists.

JIT‑SD keeps USD's evaluator and makes its inputs generated on demand and parameterised by who is asking.

Consumption
Hydra · Fabric · renderers · game runtimes
Composition
USD — LIVRPS, layers, schemas, payloads · unchanged
Resolution
JIT‑SD — contracts, channels, baselines, contexts · the new layer
Storage
Content-addressed objects + an append-only ledger

What it is not

  • Not a new scene description format. USD stays, entirely.
  • Not a competitor to glTF or USDZ. Those are output codecs — the compiled binaries. This is the compiler and the build system.
  • Not a replacement for live collaborative editing. Live sync is consumed, not replaced.
  • Not procedural-only. A hand-sculpted hero asset is a generator with no parameters. Everything is addressed as a function; constants are functions.

The structural rule everything rests on

The relational plane — parameters, ports, contracts, dependency structure — is kilobytes and never sees a triangle. The geometric plane is opaque kernels invoked at the leaves of a resolved derivation. Let geometry into the relational plane and you lose termination guarantees, cacheability, and any hope of resolving a graph in under a second.

Operationally this is also what makes it fast: resolve the whole graph from parameters and port specs alone, then fan out geometry generation in parallel. Naive fan-out gives a serial chain where tail latency sums rather than maxes.

02 — Two phases

Definition and Assembly are not film words.

Film calls them "asset build" and "shot execution." Those are not film-specific activities — they are the two halves of every USD workflow, in every industry that has adopted it.

Definition authors a reusable, context-free, timeless description of a kind of thing. A type, not an instance. Assembly binds definitions into a specific place, at a specific moment, for a specific purpose, and materialises the result.

Read the last row. Every one of these industries independently invented the same idea — a pinned coordinate saying which definitions were in force — and every one implements it as a bespoke pipeline database bolted onto USD from outside, incompatibly.

Why the non-film case is the stronger one

Outside entertainment, USD is used as a downstream assembly and visualisation target, not a source of truth. Truth stays in CAD, PLM or BIM; something converts to USD; the USD gets assembled and looked at. At that boundary you lose parametric intent (a feature tree becomes triangles), version identity, and the round trip.

CAD, PLM and BIM are already just-in-time systems. The bake to USD is what stops them being one.

A CAD model is a program that generates geometry from parameters. A Revit family is a generator that builds itself against its host. PLM effectivity is a pinned coordinate. These industries have had these ideas for thirty years in their authoring tools and discard all of it at export. Film has to be persuaded its bakes are a problem; manufacturing already knows.

03 — Versioning

Versions are acts of intent, not moments in time.

An earlier draft made the evaluation coordinate a wall-clock timestamp. That was wrong. Clock time records when but carries no meaning — which is why autosave history is excellent for recovery and useless for collaboration. There is no way to say "use the good one."

Every system people actually hand work off through versions by deliberate act: a tag, a release, a lifecycle state change, an issued drawing set. Time survives only as an index for looking those up afterwards.

Immutable

Revisions

Content-addressed, never typed by a human. Every save makes one. Never mutated, never reused, deduped by construction.

The only mutable object

Channels

@wip, @review, @approved. A pointer to a revision, owned by a team. Moving it is deliberate, attributed, audited.

Immutable

Baselines

A named, complete resolution map — the whole world pinned. A lockfile. Created by a decision, never by a clock.

There is exactly one mutable object in the system. All volatility is confined to a single small surface that is human-controlled and auditable — which is why rollback is a pointer move, not a migration.

Channels are not a new idea

Film runs work → publish → approved. Games run dev → main → cert → live. PLM runs In Work → Under Review → Released → Superseded. AEC has it standardised in ISO 19650 as Work in Progress → Shared → Published → Archived. Every industry converged on a promotion ladder and every one implements it as a status field beside files that resolution ignores. The contribution here is making the pointer be the resolution mechanism.

Float on compatibility, pin on breakage

Everyone floating on latest means nothing is stable. Everyone pinning hard means everything is stale. The way out is to couple to the contract, not the revision: a consumer floats freely across any revision still satisfying the contract it declared, and breaking changes stop at the boundary.

And unlike semver in software — which is a human promise maintainers frequently get wrong — compatibility here is measured, not asserted. Every generator ships exemplars, its canonical parameter bindings. Promotion re-evaluates every downstream consumer's exemplars and compares manifests. A "compatible" change that breaks one is reclassified as breaking and refused.

Reproducibility without discipline

You never ask for a baseline — you receive one. Resolving against floating channels mints a baseline as a side effect and hands it back, the way npm install writes a lockfile unasked. Reproducibility becomes a byproduct of working normally rather than something anyone must remember.

What people actually say

Nobody says a hash out loud. Hashes fail at both jobs a spoken version name does: ordering (v37 is obviously after v36) and pronounceability. The only number form people do speak is monotonic and centrally assigned — publish versions, revision letters, changelists, build numbers.

People name inputs and decisions. Machines identify outputs.

So what appears on screen is a coordinate, not an identity — hero_car v37 · baseline 118 · lighting. Speakable, ordered, diagnostic: two people seeing different pictures locate the disagreement in seconds. The hash lives underneath, in the receipt and the burn-in, never in anyone's mouth.

Live WIP

Live sync is a transport attached to a channel, never a requirement. Each participant authors into their own sublayer, and everyone subscribes to everyone else's deltas — which means conflict resolution comes free from LIVRPS. No CRDT, no merge algorithm; layer strength decides, deterministically, by an order declared up front and visible to everyone. Better than last-writer-wins, which is invisible.

A live session is volatile and disposable. Checkpoints crystallise it into immutable revisions, automatically every few seconds. Three invariants keep it from corrupting the reproducible core:

  • Live is viewable, never citable. You may watch a colleague's session; nothing with a receipt may be built from it. Only revisions can be resolved against.
  • Losing the live server degrades to solo, never to a blocked session.
  • Ownership is advisory, not a lock. Locking by default is how you get people emailing each other to release files.

04 — Contracts

Couple to contracts, not to names.

USD composes by namespace overlay — an over on /World/Set/Trees/Tree_047. Everyone must agree on the tree in advance, and renaming a prim breaks downstream departments. Namespace editing and relocates exist to paper over this; they are a patch on the underlying coupling.

JIT‑SD binds by structural schema match instead: the resolver finds a provider whose emitted interface satisfies a consumer's declared requirement. The namespace becomes an output of composition rather than a precondition for it. Contracts are declared as codeless applied API schemas — pure plugin data, nobody compiles anything.

Four archetypes carry most of it

ArchetypeFilmGamesManufacturingAEC
Mount / Socketrig attachmentweapon socketfastener patternfaçade grid
Field / Domainscatter densityfoliage spawnthermal fielddaylight, occupancy
Envelopesim boundsnavmesh clearanceswept volumeegress path
Kinematic chainrigsanim graphsmechanismsoperable parts

One archetype, four industries. The solver understands only archetypes; each industry's vocabulary arrives as a schema pack on top. "Wheels must match the chassis mount points" is exactly "props must sit on the terrain after FX re-sculpts it."

Ports carry envelopes, not just values

A port is a transform plus an admissible envelope. That is what actually decouples teams: a consumer iterates freely while staying inside it, and the provider never re-resolves. Only envelope violations escalate. Units live in the type system, because dimensional mismatch across a team boundary is the empirical failure mode in this domain and is cheap to make unrepresentable.

Exemplars are the test suite

Nobody can author a function blind, so a generator ships canonical parameter bindings and the authoring tool opens one to work in. The same set doubles as continuous integration for 3D content — re-evaluate downstream exemplars, diff the manifests. Today film finds out at dailies, manufacturing at a design review, AEC at clash detection or on site, games at cert. All late, by looking, with humans.

Overrides bind to declared handles

Every industry needs "this one instance is special." Today a shot-level override on /World/Char/geo/mesh.points silently detonates the moment the modeler changes topology, and nobody finds out until a render looks wrong. Bound to a declared handle, a generator change that removes it fails loudly at resolution and names its owner.

Bounds without geometry

Spatial queries matter for games, digital twins and AEC — but you cannot spatially index something you have not generated yet. So every generator must declare conservative bounds computable from parameters alone, in microseconds, never wrong in the direction that hides something. Index the bounds in the relational plane, then materialise only what intersects.

05 — Contexts

The same query, compiled differently for who is asking.

USD variantSets must be authored ahead and shipped whole. Someone must anticipate every variant, they drift out of sync, they bloat the file — and an outsourcing vendor receives the unreleased-character variant whether or not they are allowed to see it. Here, variation is compiled per request and the other variants never exist. That is a security property, not merely an efficiency one.

Context is a claim set, not a role — {department, purpose, platform, budget, clearance, jurisdiction}. Roles do not compose and explode the moment "previz artist at a vendor on a tablet" appears.

Tier 1 · Representation
LOD, tessellation, displacement, texture budget, coordinate convention, output codec. Pure negotiation. May live in a Hydra filter.
Tier 2 · Semantics
A different derivation, not a coarser one. Animator gets a proxy with a rig; lighter gets displacement and shaders but no rig; the game server gets collision hulls and nothing else.
Tier 3 · Authorization
Vendor redaction, unannounced characters, territory-restricted IP. Must be an independent deny-by-default gate — otherwise a bug in a LOD rule is a data breach.

For games, tier 2 is the headline: one authored asset, N platform compilations, consistent by construction. Today each is a separate published asset from a separate pipeline step, and they drift — the mobile LOD is two revisions behind the hero, the collision proxy no longer matches the model.

The trap: omission is not redaction

Removing a prim leaves the hole in the floor, the shadow holdout, the light rig aimed at nothing, and a bounding volume that gives away the silhouette. A decimated mesh can be partially inverted. A boolean subtraction leaves the negative of what was removed.

Compile a different program. Do not compile the full one down.

The restricted variant must come from a separate declassifying generator that provably emits only a permitted feature set. "Just decimate the real model" is the tempting and wrong architecture, and it is the kind of thing discovered after it ships.

Consistency and cache sharing from one constraint

Context may change which rules fire and which codec is emitted — never the parameters flowing through shared nodes. Declare explicit variation points; everything outside them is context-invariant. So shared subgraphs hash identically across contexts, meaning all contexts share cache and the marketing render is provably the actual asset.

Approval attaches to a derivation, not a file

Dailies sign-off, engineering change approval, issued-for-construction and cert all become "someone signed derivation H at baseline E under context C." You can prove what was approved — and "has anything drifted since?" becomes a hash comparison. Instant, for a question that currently takes a person a day and is usually answered wrong.

06 — On disk

Three stores, and only one of them is big.

A revision is a recipe, not geometry. v037.usda is about three kilobytes — a real USD layer, openable in usdview, containing prim definitions, contract declarations, parameters, and payloads pointing at object-store hashes. The four gigabytes live once, shared by every version that did not change them.

/jitsd/objects/                       immutable · content-addressed · write-once
  sha256/8f/2c/8f2c9a1b….usdc          geometry payload            (GB)
  sha256/aa/12/aa12cc90….wasm          a generator kernel          (MB)

/jitsd/ledger/                        append-only · all text · all diffable
  assets/hero_car/revisions/v037.usda  the recipe                  (KB)
  assets/hero_car/channels/approved    → v036   the only mutable thing
  baselines/000118.usda                "client_review"             (KB)

~/work/seq040_sh0120/                 per person · disposable
  shot.usda                            what the artist edits       (KB)
  jitsd.lock                           resolved against 118        (KB)
  .jitsd/cache/                        hardlinks. delete anytime.  (GB)

JIT‑SD moves the source of truth from the big files to the small ones.

Today the precious artifact is a 4 GB file that must never be deleted. Here it is a 3 KB recipe, and the 4 GB is a cache anyone can rebuild. Saving writes a hash, uploads the blob only if new, writes a 3 KB revision, and moves a pointer. That pointer move is the only thing in the entire system that overwrites anything.

Merkle the hierarchy

Hash every node so a node's hash includes its children's. Subtree equality becomes one comparison rather than forty thousand prim visits; diff cost becomes proportional to changes rather than scene size — exactly why git status is instant on a huge repo. This is a core structural decision, not an optimisation: the kilobyte-scale drift check only works if it is in place.

What OpenVDB already proved

Two ideas transfer directly. Tiles — an internal node holding one constant instead of a child pointer — mean a 200,000-prim plant does not need a 200,000-row pin table. Topology separated from values gives the sharper formulation of the two-plane rule: a baseline diff is a topological operation, not a value comparison. And VDB's value accessors, which cache the root-to-leaf path, are the model for caching derivation paths so a lighter working on one character re-resolves nothing frame to frame.

But the strongest signal is NanoVDB. Three independent teams converged on the same split:

SystemAuthoring representationConsumption representation
OpenVDBmutable pointer treeNanoVDB
USDUsdStage + compositionFabric
JIT‑SDderivation + baselinematerialised payload

Authoring structure and consumption structure should be different data structures, and the compile between them is where the value is.

07 — Relation to existing work

What this competes with, and what it does not.

Plain USD — filling a gap it left deliberately

USD has no concept of "as of," no contract system, no provenance beyond which layer won, no way to detect breakage except looking, and no redaction model. But these are not failures. USD was scoped as an interchange and composition format, leaving pipeline concerns to the studio, and at that scope it succeeded more completely than anything before it. Pixar had a pipeline team to fill the empty layer.

The problem is that USD's success carried it into industries with no Pixar-scale pipeline team, each now rebuilding that layer badly and incompatibly. This is a proposal to standardise the layer USD deliberately left empty — not a criticism of what USD chose to do.

Omniverse / Nucleus — complementary, at a different timescale

Nucleus with live layers is a push model: state lives on a server, clients mutate in place, deltas broadcast. JIT‑SD is a pull model: nothing is mutated, immutable revisions are created and pointers move deliberately.

Live sync is what happens inside a revision. JIT‑SD is what happens between revisions.

These compose. A team collaborates live on @wip using delta sync; on promotion to @review, the immutable revision, contract check and exemplar validation engage. Do not compete on live multi-user editing — pull-based resolution is genuinely worse at four people rearranging a room together, and pretending otherwise would be a mistake.

Fabric — a materialisation target, not a rival

Fabric exists because UsdStage is optimised for authoring, not for touching values a hundred times a second. It answers "how do I read this at frame rate." JIT‑SD answers "which scene, from whose definitions, at what baseline, compiled for whom." The natural pipeline runs one into the other. Fabric actually strengthens the case: it proves the industry already accepts that the runtime representation should be a compiled derivative rather than the authored one.

08 — Adoption

It has to work like git init.

The cold-start problem is the serious risk: value comes from teams coupling through contracts, so one team adopting in isolation gets almost nothing, and network-effect systems die in that gap. The constraint that answers it:

No rung may be required for a lower rung to be useful.

01Solo. Local object store and ledger, auto-versioning, baselines.nothing
02Solo, many machines. A remote; push and pull revisions.a blob store
03Small team, one asset. Live sync on a WIP channel.a delta relay
04Multiple teams. Channels, promotion, contracts.+ a sequencer
05Cross-org. Contexts, redaction, exemplar CI.+ policy, workers

What rung one gives one person, alone, offline

Every save versioned forever, with no _v3_final_FINAL. Instant rollback as a pointer move. Reproducible renders, because "why does this frame look different from yesterday's" is a daily problem for one person too. Named checkpoints. Structural diff.

And storage collapse — measured, not assumed. Twelve saves of a 16 MiB asset under localised editing cost 1.14× one copy. On real USD crate, editing one mesh in a 4000-mesh scene costs 0.1% of the file, and reordering every prim costs 1.3%. The caveat is honest: a re-export that perturbs every value deduplicates nothing, and no chunker can see through that.

That is a defensible standalone product with zero collaborators, zero contracts and zero servers. Contracts become an upgrade earned once a second team appears.

Where this is the wrong paradigm

For a locked shot or a shipped level, bake it. The real home is assets in flux with many authors — configurators, engineering review, simulation prep, live product data, anything with a supply chain. But a bake is just a query pinned to a baseline and flattened, so it falls out for free, and the file is demoted from source of truth to cached result. Far easier to sell than "files are obsolete," with the advantage of being true.

09 — What measurement corrected

Five claims on this page were wrong until something ran.

Everything above was argued before any of it was built. A working reference implementation — 76 tests, real USD in the loop — then contradicted it in five places. The corrections are more useful than the original claims, so they are recorded rather than quietly patched.

Storage
Whole-file content addressing deduplicates nothing. One changed byte changes the file hash, so "200 saves of a 4 GB asset cost 4 GB plus deltas" was simply false as written — it would cost 800 GB. The claim needs content-defined chunking, which is a hard requirement rather than an optimisation, and was absent from the design entirely.
Chunk size
The default was wrong by an order of magnitude. 1 MiB chunks against real USD crate cost 12.5% of the file for a one-mesh edit and 67% for a prim reorder. At the measured knee of 64 KiB: 0.31% and 2.5%. The strategy was fine; the parameter was not.
Determinism
Crate output is deterministic. This page asserted USD gives no byte-stable serialisation. Written twice, the same scene produced byte-identical files. The claim holds across USD versions and writers — not within one, which is where it was being applied.
Archetypes
Three of four duplicated USD. UsdPhysics already models joints, limits, drives and articulation roots; a primvar with a declared interpolation already is a sampleable surface field; extentsHint already provides bounds readable without loading payloads. The kinematic archetype was deleted outright and the rest reduced to thin markers over existing schemas.
Syntax
Five USD syntax errors, two in shipping code. Installing real USD found payload emitted in the prim body rather than its metadata block, an over written with no braces, and uniform rel. This page had claimed for weeks that the design "fits USD". It mostly did — but only real USD could say where it did not.

Two of the alarming numbers that drove design decisions turned out to be measurement errors of my own making — a synthetic worst case that no USD workflow produces, and a chunk size larger than the file being chunked.

The pattern worth carrying: ideas borrowed from Nix, git and OpenVDB kept arriving disguised as new, when USD already had them under different names. Sparse layers are tile collapse. (path, field) is per-attribute decomposition. The audit that catches this is cheap; not running it is expensive.

10 — Open questions

What is still undecided.

  1. Channel ownership and promotion policyWhether promotion is a permission check, an exemplar gate, or a human sign-off. This is where the design stops being a data architecture and becomes an organisational one.
  2. Checkpoint granularity in a live sessionOne co-authored revision, or per-participant sublayer revisions that compose? Decides whether authorship is per-asset or per-layer, and what "revert" means when three people are in the room.
  3. Bounds as a function of parametersNarrowed by the audit: extentsHint already gives bounds without loading payloads. What is missing is that it is authored data rather than a cheap evaluation. What form does that function take, and who writes it?
  4. Contract versioning semanticsAdding an optional port is compatible; removing one is breaking; tightening a tolerance envelope is breaking for some consumers and not others. How much can the exemplar suite decide automatically?
  5. Diamond dependenciesTwo consumers need incompatible revisions of the same provider. Package managers have twenty years of answers — which apply when the package is geometry that must be spatially coherent with itself?
  6. A real ArResolverEverything emitted opens in USD, but a resolver plugin is C++, so usdview cannot yet open a jitsd:// path and receive a compiled stage. That is the remaining half of the integration.
  7. Live contract validationShould a participant see "you have just broken the wheel team's mount" as they work? Valuable, but it puts relational evaluation on the interactive path.
